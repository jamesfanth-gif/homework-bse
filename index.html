Question 1: Low-Pressure Steam Piping Arrangements based on Gravity Return 20 Marks

1. Steam Heating System & Gravity Return

A steam heating system supplies heat to a space by connecting a source of steam through piping to heat-emitting equipment (radiators) in the space. The steam gives up its heat in the radiators and condenses into water (condensate).

In a gravity return system, steam rises by natural buoyancy while condensate flows back to the boiler by gravity alone without any mechanical pump.

2. System Classification

There are two (2) common designs of low-pressure steam systems based on gravity return:

  1. Single-pipe gravity return system
  2. 2-pipe gravity return system

For the 2-pipe system, there are two possible return pipe arrangements:

  • (a) Dry return: Condensate pipework is installed above the boiler water level.
  • (b) Wet return: Condensate pipework is installed below the boiler water level.

3. Operating Principles

  • Single-pipe gravity return system: A single pipe serves both steam supply and condensate return. Steam travels along the upper section of the pitched main, while condensate drains back along the bottom in the opposite direction. Automatic air vents are installed on radiators and at the end of the main to eliminate air binding.
  • 2-pipe gravity return system: Steam supply and condensate return are separated into two distinct pipe circuits. Radiators are connected in parallel; each unit receives steam through a supply riser and discharges condensate through a return drop. In a wet return, a check valve is installed near the boiler to prevent boiler water backflow.

4. Advantages & Disadvantages of Steam Systems

  • Advantages: Higher heat carrying capacity (sensible and latent heat); suitable for long-distance transport; lower running cost due to large-scale production.
  • Disadvantages: Condensate disposal and air elimination from lengthy pipelines are the two major problems.
Question 2: Energy Saving vs. Energy Recovery & 3-D Devices 30 Marks

1. Concepts of Energy Conservation

  • Energy Saving: Using more efficient methods and equipment to limit the amounts of energy used. Examples: high-efficiency motors, fans, and pumps; chillers of higher COP; building envelopes with better thermal insulation.
  • Energy Recovery: Capturing waste heat produced by systems and putting it to other uses. Examples: heat pipe heat exchangers, enthalpy wheels, run-around coils, heat reclaim condensers, and heat pumps.

2. Device 1: Heat Pipe Heat Exchanger (Gas-to-Gas)

  • Appearance & Construction (3-D): A rectangular casing housing an array of individual, permanently sealed finned tubes. A sealed partition plate separates the counterflow exhaust and supply air streams, completely preventing cross-contamination.
  • Working Principle: Operates on a closed-loop evaporation/condensation cycle. Thermal energy from the hot exhaust vaporizes fluid at the hot end; vapour migrates to the cooler end, condenses, and releases latent heat to the cold incoming air. Liquid returns via an internal capillary wick and gravity. No moving parts and no external power required.
  • Factors Affecting Capacity: Wick design, tube diameter (15–50 mm), working fluid properties, temperature difference, and inclination tilt angle.
  • Disadvantage: Transfers sensible heat only; cannot recover latent heat.

3. Device 2: Enthalpy Wheel (Heat Wheel)

  • Appearance & Construction (3-D): Consists of a slowly rotating porous wheel packed with heat-and-moisture absorbing honeycomb matrix, drive mechanism, casing, air seals, and counterflow ductwork.
  • Working Principle: The wheel rotates slowly between the exhaust and supply air streams. In winter, sensible heat and latent heat (moisture) from the warm exhaust air are picked up by the matrix, then transferred to the cold incoming fresh air as the wheel rotates through it. Regeneration is continuous.
  • Efficiency & Merits: Recovers 70% to 90% of exhaust energy, transferring both sensible and latent heat.
  • Disadvantages: Bulky cross-section (2×2 m to 5×5 m); complex ductwork arrangement; requires routine maintenance; slight risk of cross-contamination (minimized by purge units).
Question 3: Dalton's Law, Moist Air Properties & Psychrometric Chart 20 Marks

1. Dalton's Law

Dalton's law states that the total pressure of a gas mixture equals the sum of the partial pressures of the individual elements occupying the same volume at the same temperature:

Pm = P1 + P2 + P3 + ... + Pn

Applying Dalton's law to moist air (dry air + water vapour):

Pat = Pa + Pw

where: Pat = atmospheric pressure of moist air, Pa = partial pressure of dry air, and Pw = partial pressure of water vapour.

2. Eleven (11) Properties of Moist Air

  1. Humidity ratio (w): Ratio between mass of water vapour mw and dry air ma:
    w = mw / ma = 0.62198 · Pw / (Pat - Pw)   [kg/kg da]
  2. Dry bulb temperature (t): Moist air temperature measured by a standard thermometer (°C).
  3. Wet bulb temperature (t*): Measured by a thermometer wrapped in a wetted wick with 1–2 m/s air velocity (°C).
  4. Dew point temperature (td): Temperature at which condensation of moisture begins (°C).
  5. Moisture content: Weight of water vapour in kg per kg of dry air (kg/kg da).
  6. Relative humidity (φ): Ratio of actual water vapour partial pressure to saturated vapour pressure at the same temperature (%).
  7. Degree of saturation (μ): Ratio of humidity ratio w to saturated humidity ratio ws at the same temperature and pressure (μ = w / ws).
  8. Specific volume (v): Volume of moist air in m3 per kg of dry air (m3/kg da).
  9. Enthalpy (h): Amount of heat in moist air above datum (-17.8°C for dry air, 0°C water for vapour):
    h = 1.005 t + w(2501 + 1.87 t)   [kJ/kg]
  10. Sensible Heat Ratio (SHR): Sensible Heat / (Sensible Heat + Latent Heat).
  11. Room Sensible Heat Ratio (RSHR): Room Sensible / (Room Sensible + Room Latent Heat).

3. Six (6) Thermodynamic Properties on Psychrometric Chart

  1. Dry-bulb temperature (θ): Vertical lines (bottom horizontal axis, °C).
  2. Wet-bulb temperature (θ'): Diagonal lines sloping downward to the right (°C).
  3. Moisture content (g): Horizontal lines (right vertical axis, kg/kg da).
  4. Specific enthalpy (h): Diagonal lines sloping upward to the left (kJ/kg).
  5. Specific volume (v): Steeply inclined lines leaning to the left (m3/kg).
  6. Relative humidity (H): Curved lines curving upward from the saturation curve (%).
Question 4: Bypass Factor & Five Air Conditioning Processes 30 Marks

1. Bypass Factor of Cooling Coil

  • Operating Principle: Bypass factor is a function of physical and operating characteristics of the cooling coil and represents the portion of air which is considered to pass through the cooling coil without change of its operating condition.
  • Affecting Conditions:
    • Decrease of heat transfer surface (fewer coil rows, fewer fins/cm, wider tube spacing) → Increases Bypass Factor.
    • Decrease of air velocity (providing more contact residence time) → Decreases Bypass Factor.
Bypass Factor = (to - ts) / (tm - ts) = (ho - hs) / (hm - hs) = (wo - ws) / (wm - ws)

where: tm, hm, wm = entering mixing air; to, ho, wo = off-coil condition; ts, hs, ws = apparatus dew point (ADP).

2. The Five (5) Air Conditioning Processes

(1) Cooling vs. Dehumidification (Cooling + Dehumidification)
Sensible cooling alone is a horizontal line moving left (w constant), whereas pure dehumidification is a vertical downward line (t constant). In practical operation, air passes over a cooling coil with surface temperature below entering dew point (Td); hence sensible cooling and dehumidification occur simultaneously. t, w, and h all decrease.
Line on Chart: Straight line sloping downwards and to the left (1 → 2) towards the saturation curve.

(2) Heating vs. Humidification (Heating + Humidification)
Sensible heating alone is a horizontal line moving right (w constant, t and h increase). Humidification adds moisture. In the combined process, air is first heated sensibly from T1 to T2 (1 → 2), then humidified via steam or water spray (2 → 3). Effectiveness: E = (h3 - h2) / (hs - h2).
Line on Chart: 1 → 2 is horizontal to the right; 2 → 3 slopes upwards and to the left towards saturation.

(3) Cooling + Dehumidification + Reheat
Air is first cooled and dehumidified below dew point (1 → 2) to remove moisture, and then sensibly reheated (2 → 3) to comfort supply temperature without adding moisture. Prevents space overcooling.
Line on Chart: 1 → 2 slopes downwards and to the left; 2 → 3 is strictly horizontal to the right (w constant).

(4) Evaporative Cooling
Simultaneous removal of sensible heat and addition of moisture along constant wet-bulb temperature line without external heat addition or removal (Ideal Adiabatic Saturation Process).
Line on Chart: Moves upwards and to the left along constant wet-bulb line (1 → 2) towards saturation (t decreases, w increases).

(5) Air Mixing Process
Return air (1) and outdoor fresh air (o) mix adiabatically in the AHU mixing chamber. The mixture point (m) always lies on the straight line connecting 1 and o.
% of re-circulation air = x / y  |  % of outdoor air = (y - x) / y
Line on Chart: A straight line joining point 1 and point o, with mixing point m on the segment.

扣分死穴 1：Q1 重力回水系統誤加電動冷凝水泵（Condensate Pump）
題目指明是 Gravity Return（重力回水）。重力回水純靠蒸氣自然上升及凝結水重力回流，完全無泵！原答案在文字和圖中加入了 pump，直接違背工程定義。
扣分死穴 2：Q4(2) 加熱過程方向畫成垂直向上
在心理濕度圖上，垂直向上是「等溫加濕」。加熱是乾球溫度升高、水分不變，必須水平向右 (1 → 2)！
扣分死穴 3：Q4(3) 再熱（Reheat）方向畫成垂直向上
再熱純粹是顯熱升溫，水分含量保持不變，必須是水平向右 (2 → 3)。
扣分死穴 4：Q4(4) 蒸發冷卻箭頭完全反向
蒸發冷卻是溫度下降、含濕量增加，箭頭必須沿濕球線向左上方靠近飽和線！原答案畫向右下角完全顛倒。
💡 術語與公式大白話解讀（睇明佢就唔會驚）
1. Pat = Pa + Pw（道爾頓分壓定律）
• Pat：大氣總壓力。
• Pa：乾空氣自己產生的壓力。
• Pw：水蒸氣自己產生的壓力。
👉 白話意思： 空氣由乾空氣和水蒸氣組成，總大氣壓就等於兩者各自壓力相加。
2. m3/kg da（比容 Specific Volume 的單位）
• da：Dry Air（乾空氣）。
👉 白話意思： 「每 1 公斤乾空氣，在空間中霸佔了多少立方米體積」。
3. μ = w / ws（飽和度 Degree of Saturation）
• w：現在空氣中實際含水量。
• ws：同溫度下，空氣極限最多能裝的水量。
👉 白話意思： 水杯最多裝 100ml（ws），現在倒了 60ml（w），飽和度就是 60/100 = 0.6。
4. 點解純加熱是水平向右？
在濕度圖上，橫軸是溫度（越右越熱），縱軸是水分（越上越多水）。電暖爐純吹熱風，只升溫度（向右行），但完全無加水（高度不變），所以必然是「水平向右直線」。
